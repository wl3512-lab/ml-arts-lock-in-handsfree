Summary

lock in is a study timer I use every day: a Mac menu bar app with focus and break rounds, themes, sounds and share codes, built from my friend Braxton Hauw's web timer. It only knows what I tell it, and the three things I have to tell it are exactly the ones I forget: press start, press pause when I get up, and put my phone down.

For this final I gave it a camera and one rule: the machine learning may only remove a step, never add one. No forms, no task picker, no ratings. A Teachable Machine image model, trained on photos of me studying, tells working from holding my phone. A pretrained pose model (ml5 bodyPose, MoveNet) tells whether anyone is in the chair at all. Small rules on top turn those guesses into timer actions. Sit down and work for 5 seconds and a cancelable "starting in 5…" begins focus. Leave for 10 seconds and it pauses and takes those seconds back. Hold your phone for 20 seconds and the ring turns yellow and pulses, with one notification. It never pauses for the phone.

Most of the work turned out to be about what happens when the model is wrong, and I found the wrong moments by recording myself studying. An empty room read as "phone 100%". The model read "working" for two seconds in the middle of scrolling and cleared the phone alert. bodyPose lost me for single frames and kept restarting the start countdown. Each recording turned into a rule, a retrained model or a test. I trained four models in different places and outfits, and built a compare page that runs every model on the same photos and counts its confident mistakes.

Two of the biggest fixes weren't about training at all. ml5 was handing my model a squashed, un-mirrored picture instead of the mirrored square Teachable Machine trained on. And ml5 stopped guessing whenever the tab was in the background, which is exactly when the phone alert matters. My answer to the week 8 question, train your own or use pretrained, is both: pretrained for "is anyone here", mine for "is that a phone".

The lock in menu bar app owns the timer. When a focus round starts it opens the sketch in watch mode, and the camera is on only during focus. Frames never leave the Mac.

Inspiration

  • My own week. Sessions that said I focused for 50 minutes when I know I didn't.
  • Calm technology. Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown, "Designing Calm Technology" (1995): technology that stays in the periphery and only moves to the center when needed.
  • iPhone's attention-aware features. Machine learning you never configure, which only removes steps.
  • Focus apps like Forest, as the opposite: they make you press a button and punish you for leaving.
  • Teachable Machine (week 2), which makes retraining on your own desk a few minutes of work.

Recording myself. I added a test log (?trace=1) that writes down every half second what each model saw and what the timer did. Three recordings became three fixes:

  • An empty room the model had never seen read "phone 100%". Teachable Machine has to pick one of its three classes, and nothing in training looked like that room. Now bodyPose decides whether anyone is here, and my model only decides working vs phone. A confident "phone" still wins, because a phone held up can hide my face from bodyPose.
  • While I was still scrolling, the model read "working" for about two seconds and "phone down?" cleared. Now misreads under 4 seconds don't restart the 20 second count, and the alert clears only after 6 seconds with no phone at all.
  • My model said "working" on every frame, but bodyPose missed me for single frames, which flipped the guess to "away 100%" and kept restarting the start countdown. Now "nobody here" needs 1.5 seconds of misses.

The model was seeing a different picture. Teachable Machine's webcam records a mirrored square from the middle of the frame. When I read ml5 1.4.0's source, its image classifier ignored the flipped option for Teachable Machine models and stretched the whole 4:3 frame into a square, so every model was judging a squashed, un-mirrored version of me. The sketch now cuts the same mirrored square Teachable Machine trained on and runs the model itself. ?raw=1 keeps the old way, so I can compare the two.

The background tab. "phone down?" matters most when I'm in another tab. But ml5's classify() waits for an animation frame before every guess, and Chrome gives a hidden tab none. In a browser test with animation frames stopped, the old path answered 0 times in 6 seconds and the new one kept guessing.

Nobody in frame. I checked what each model says when there's nothing to see: a blank wall, a dark room, a bright frame. None of them says "away". Model 2 says working 89–93%, model 1 says 71–90% and model 3 says 57–61%. On its own, my model can't spot an empty desk, so bodyPose has to stay.

Easy: training in Teachable Machine, the timer rules (pure JavaScript with 13 tests, no camera needed) and the yellow pulse. Hard: anything that changes the picture: a new room, new light, my phone against a dark sweater, and the tab going into the background. The numbers for those don't come from training. They come from recording myself and counting.

Audience and context

First, me, as an audience of one, at my desk and in the places I actually study. Then three friends who already study with me on lock in share codes. The goal is that people barely interact with it. You sit down, and it's already counting. The only taps are corrections: canceling a start you didn't want, or "I was here" after a wrong pause. I count every correction, because a correction is a step the ML added back.

It knows if you're here, not if you're on task. A camera can't see your screen, and I'd rather say that plainly than pretend. It's a camera pointed at you while you study, so it's on only during focus rounds, frames never leave the computer, and the training photos are saved only when I turn on ?collect=.
